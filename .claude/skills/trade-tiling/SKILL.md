# trade-tiling — research playbook

Use for any task that involves laying tiles or similar rigid floor/wall coverings. It tells a researcher
**what to find out and where to confirm it**. It deliberately contains no technical values: every number,
class, time or limit in the artifact must come from a source opened during this run (`research-evidence`).

## Recommended research split

When the coordinator splits the research into parallel nodes, use these topics (slug → artifact):

| Slug | Scope |
|---|---|
| `substrate` | Base assessment and preparation, leveling/flatness, moisture, cracks, dusting concrete, primer, movement joints, tile leveling system (SVP) and its limits |
| `bonding` | Adhesive class and suitability, application method and coverage for the format, grout type and chemical/frost resistance, joint width, temperature and curing windows, waiting times and load-bearing times, cold-weather limits |
| `safety` | Dust (silica) and respiratory protection, eye/hearing/hand/knee protection, ventilation, electrical limits of the master's lines, lifting and handling of large-format tiles, chemical hazards of primers and epoxy products |

A single-node research covers all three in that order.

## Question checklist (must be answered or explicitly listed as unknown)

### substrate
- How is the existing base judged for tile (strength, cleanliness, dusting, cracks, flatness over a given
  straightedge length) and by which tests or inspections?
- What preparation fits this kind of base (mechanical, primer, repair of cracks, leveling compound)?
- Moisture: how is it checked for a slab on ground and what are the risks if skipped?
- Movement and perimeter joints: which are required, where, and with which materials?
- What does a tile leveling system do and not do (it does not fix an uneven base)? Clip/wedge
  compatibility with tile thickness and joint width; reuse; removal timing and its dependence on
  temperature.

### bonding
- Which adhesive class and properties are needed for: this tile type and format, this load (vehicle), this
  environment (cold, wet, de-icing salt), this base?
- Application method and coverage for large format (notched trowel size, back-buttering) and what the
  master's existing trowel can do.
- Grout: cementitious vs epoxy for these conditions; joint width for rectified tiles of this size;
  required movement-joint filling.
- Temperature: minimum substrate and air temperature for primer, adhesive, grout; what happens below it;
  how curing time scales; the practical decision rule "lay / postpone".
- Waiting times: primer, adhesive pot life and open time, walkable time, wedge removal, grouting, full
  load (vehicle).

### safety
- Silica dust from cutting and from dusting concrete: exposure limits in the master's country, wet cutting,
  extraction class of the vacuum, respirator class.
- PPE per operation (cutting, mixing, wedge removal, grinding).
- Power limits for the tools to be used at once on the master's circuit; extension and mixer specifics.
- Handling large and heavy tiles alone; ergonomics.
- Chemical hazards of the chosen primer, adhesive and epoxy grout (ventilation, skin contact).

## Typical mistakes to verify against sources

- Laying on a dusting or unsealed base without primer.
- Skipping coverage checks (hollow spots under large tiles).
- Ignoring temperature limits during curing, not only during laying.
- Grouting or loading before the waiting time.
- Treating a leveling system as a substitute for base flatness.
- No movement joints on a large area or at the perimeter.

## Source pointers (types, to be confirmed by search)

- Manufacturer technical data sheets for adhesives, primers, grouts and leveling systems (local brands
  available in the master's country first).
- European classification standards for adhesives and grouts and the national occupational-exposure
  regulation of the master's country.
- Local trade and construction publications; large local DIY retailers for availability and prices
  (prices belong to the product research, not to this research).
