# trade-carpentry — research playbook

Use for any task that builds or mounts furniture or woodwork. It tells a researcher **what to find out and
where to confirm it**. It deliberately contains no technical values: every number, class or limit in the
artifact must come from a source opened during this run (`research-evidence`).

## Questions for the master (requirements stage)

Used by the requirements step. Ask only what the request, the profile and the inventory do not already
answer, at most three at a time, each with a proposed default. They are questions, not facts.

- What exactly is being made and what must it carry or do: the kind and approximate weight of the load
  (books, tools, clothes), the number of shelves or compartments?
- Size and place: length, depth, height, where it goes, and anything it must fit around.
- The wall or base it is fixed to: material and condition (concrete, brick, hollow block, plasterboard,
  timber studs, tiled), and whether cables or pipes may run behind it?
- Look and material: solid wood, plywood or board material, finish, visible or hidden fixings, any
  boards or hardware already owned?
- Tools and help: which of the owned tools are to be used, is a panel-cutting service available?
- Budget limit, time frame, and whether a ready-made equivalent is an acceptable comparison.

## Recommended research split

| Slug | Scope |
|---|---|
| `materials` | Board and timber materials (solid wood, plywood, MDF, chipboard, edge finishing), thickness and span/deflection for the intended load, joinery and construction method, hardware (hinges, drawer slides, brackets, connectors), finishing |
| `mounting` | Fixing to walls and floors: wall/base type identification, anchors and fasteners per base, load capacity and safety factor, studs/rails/cleats, anti-tip fixing for tall units |
| `safety` | Dust (wood, MDF), machine safety for the tools used, hearing/eye/respiratory protection, electrical limits of the master's lines, handling of sheet material, finishing-product hazards |

A single-node research covers all three in that order. Skip `mounting` when nothing is wall- or floor-fixed.

## Question checklist (must be answered or explicitly listed as unknown)

### materials
- Which material and thickness suit the use (load, humidity, visible or hidden, finish) and the master's
  tools and skills?
- Span and sag for shelves and wide panels under the stated load; when a stiffener or support is needed.
- Which joints and assembly method fit the tools the master owns (e.g. what the router, saw, drill can do)?
- Hardware: types and the parameters that must match (load rating, opening angle, slide length, overlay),
  and what to check against the chosen board thickness.
- Edge treatment and finishing: options, compatibility with the material, drying and recoat waiting times,
  temperature limits.
- Which cuts the local panel-cutting service can do (the master uses such services for cutting lists;
  note what to specify to them, do not produce a cutting plan).

### mounting
- How to identify the wall or base (concrete, brick, hollow block, plasterboard, timber) and verify it.
- Which fastener type per base; embedment and edge-distance rules; how load rating is derived for a
  multi-fastener fixing.
- Load case: static load, dynamic load, lever arm; safety margin and what the manufacturer data sheets say.
- Anti-tip and wall fixing for tall furniture.
- Where hidden utilities may run (electrical, water) and how to check before drilling.

### safety
- Wood and MDF dust: exposure limits in the master's country, extraction class, respirator.
- Router, circular saw and sander hazards, kickback, workpiece clamping; use of the tools the master owns.
- PPE per operation; hearing and eye protection.
- Power limits for the tools used on the master's circuit.
- Finishing chemicals: ventilation, flammable waste (oily rags), skin contact.

## Typical mistakes to verify against sources

- Underestimating shelf sag and fixing capacity; fixing into an unverified base.
- Hardware that does not match board thickness or overlay.
- Ignoring wood movement and humidity for solid wood.
- Not accounting for the kerf and thickness in cutting lists.
- Router bits whose shank does not fit the owned collet.
- Skipping recoat times and temperature limits of the finish.

## Source pointers (types, to be confirmed by search)

- Manufacturer data sheets for anchors, hardware and finishing products.
- European standards and national regulations on furniture safety and occupational exposure of the
  master's country.
- Local woodworking and construction publications; local board and hardware retailers (availability and
  prices belong to the product research, not to this research).
